# Commercial ASVAB Study Guides, 2024–2026: What They Claim the Test Emphasises, and How They Write Items

**Purpose.** This is a source-audit for an ASVAB study app used by real 2026 candidates. It records (a) which commercial guides are actually current, (b) what each publishes as the test's content weighting, (c) the facts and formulas they insist you memorise, (d) how their items are built — stems, options, distractor logic, (e) what they say the real test feels like, and (f) where they are wrong or stale.

**Research date:** 26 October 2026 (pages were fetched live on this date; page text quoted below is as served on that date).

---

## 0. Claim-tag convention (read this first)

Every claim carries one of these tags:

| Tag | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **[VERIFIED]** | I fetched the page/PDF myself and the statement is on it, **with URL**. If the statement is *about the real ASVAB*, it has also been cross-checked against an official ASVAB Program document, and the official document is cited too. |
| **[SECONDARY]** | Single-source, or third-party/blog/marketing assertion, or a claim about the test that only a prep vendor makes. Directionally useful, not authoritative. |
| **[DERIVED]** | My own arithmetic or analysis on top of verified inputs. The inputs are cited; the conclusion is mine. |
| **[UNVERIFIED]** | Could not confirm; treat as hypothesis. Says so explicitly rather than guessing. |

Two hard rules I applied:

1. **No commercial question is reproduced.** Where I describe item style I use **ILLUSTRATIVE (our construction)** examples written from scratch. Only **publicly released U.S. Government sample items** on officialasvab.com are quoted, and only in short fragments, attributed.
2. **Percentages and counts that guides publish often describe different things** (share of *items* vs share of *score* vs share of *practice questions*). I keep those separate — see §8.

**Access note.** kaptest.com, simonandschuster.com, petersons.com, mheducation.com and dummies.com were fetched directly; study.com served a CAPTCHA to every attempt, so **no Study.com claim below is tagged better than [SECONDARY]**, and I say so where relevant.

| Subtest (CAT) | 2020 fact sheet | 2023 fact sheet | 2024 | 2025 / 2026 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GS | 15 q / **10 min** | 15 q / 12 min | 12 min | 12 min |
| MK | 15 q / **23 min** | 15 q / **31 min** | 31 min | 31 min |

[VERIFIED] fact sheets for 2020, 2023, 2024, 2025 and 2026 as archived under officialasvab.com; I compared the four printings I hold (fs2020, fs2023, fs2024, fs2025 and the 2026 file). Two findings:

- **Item counts (15/15/10) have been stable since at least the 2020 printing** — so a guide publishing "16 questions" is stale by six or more years (§7.1).
- **The 2024 printing's own total is arithmetically wrong**: its subtest minutes sum to **197**, but it prints **198**. The 2025 and 2026 printings correct it to 197. [DERIVED from the printed table]. Even the official document has had an arithmetic slip; guides that copy it inherit the error.

### 2.4 Guessing: the two versions differ, and guides blur it

- The 2026 fact sheet is explicit: **"If you are running out of time, it is best to continue trying to answer as best as you can rather than filling in random guesses for the remaining items, as the CAT-ASVAB has a penalty for guessing."** [VERIFIED] 2026 fact sheet, p.1.
- Dummies' current cheat sheet says "The ASVAB doesn't penalize you for guessing (**unless you're making a lot of guesses at the end of a computerized subtest**)" [VERIFIED] https://www.dummies.com/article/asvab-for-dummies-cheat-sheet-208900 — **a partial contradiction**: the official sentence is not conditioned on "end of subtest", and it is a scoring penalty on the estimate, not a deduction per item. See §8.
- P&P: "no penalty for guessing… fill in random guesses" [VERIFIED] fact sheet.

### 2.5 The ASVAB Program does not endorse any of these books

Official statement (updated 2025-06-09): the ASVAB Enlistment Testing Program does **NOT** "recommend specific commercial ASVAB prep courses or study guides"; the only preparation it points to is school coursework, the free sample questions on officialasvab.com and asvabprogram.com, and the Army's march2success.com. It also warns that no third party has real test questions. [VERIFIED] https://www.officialasvab.com/applicants/asvab-test-preparation-disclaimer/

**Implication for the app:** "ASVAB For Dummies is recommended by recruiters nationwide" (Wiley's own blurb [VERIFIED]) is marketing; the government's position is that it recommends nothing. Do not repeat vendor authority claims as if they were endorsements.

### 2.6 What the real test actually asks — the official sample items

## 3. What guides publish as "topic weighting" — and the honest answer

**Finding (important):** **No major commercial guide publishes a percentage weighting for the eight non-AFQT subtests.** I checked the current free pages of Dummies, Kaplan, Mometrix (including its 9-page checklist PDF), Union Test Prep, Peterson's, Achievable, ASVAB Tutor and the Barron's and Simon and Schuster listings. What they publish instead is:

1. a **topic inventory** (Mometrix checklists; Kaplan's "three broad categories" for GS),
2. **item counts and time limits** (Dummies, Kaplan, ASVAB Tutor, Achievable),
3. **share of the AFQT item pool** — only Achievable does this (see §8).

Because percentages do not exist, the only defensible weighting is **derived from official item counts**:

| Subtest | Share of the 135 scored CAT items | Share of AFQT raw weight (2 x VE + AR + MK, VE = WK + PC) |
|---|---|---|
| GS | 11.1% | — (not an AFQT subtest) |
| **AR** | 11.1% | **16.7%** |
| **WK** | 11.1% | **33.3%** (double-weighted inside VE) |
| **PC** | 7.4% | **22.2%** (double-weighted inside VE) |
| **MK** | 11.1% | **16.7%** |
| EI | 11.1% | — |
| AI | 7.4% | — |
| SI | 7.4% | — |
| MC | 11.1% | — |
| AO | 11.1% | — |
| **Verbal (WK + PC)** | 18.5% of items | **55.6% of AFQT raw weight** |

[DERIVED], from the official AFQT definition **AFQT = 2 x VE + AR + MK, VE = WK + PC** [VERIFIED] https://achievable.me/exams/asvab/exam-info/ and https://www.honestmos.com/tools/asvab-line-scores (both state the same formula), combined with the official item counts in §2.1.

**The single most under-published fact in every guide I read:** for the CAT-ASVAB the two verbal subtests are **55.6% of AFQT raw weight** (on P&P it is 64.5%), while AR and MK are 16.7% each. Guides tell you to "study math" because math is where candidates improve fastest, and that advice is sound — but **WK/PC is where the points actually are**, and WK is the cheapest subtest to move (36 seconds per item, pure vocabulary). An app should surface this arithmetic explicitly.

### 4.1 GS — General Science

**(a) Guide coverage.** Kaplan frames GS as "a grab bag… you'll never be expected to know too much about a single topic", split into **life science, Earth and space science, physical science** — and states you must answer in "roughly half a minute" [VERIFIED] https://www.kaptest.com/study/asvab/whats-on-the-asvab-technical-subtests/. Dummies' GS piece says the subtest "requires a lot of straight-up memorization" and recommends learning principles rather than isolated facts [VERIFIED] https://www.dummies.com/article/academics-the-arts/study-skills-test-prep/armed-services/tips-improving-asvab-general-science-subtest-score-221205/. **Mometrix is the only vendor that publishes an itemised inventory** (2026 checklist): life science (cell structure, prokaryote vs eukaryote, organelles, DNA, Mendelian genetics, evolution and natural selection, classification, ecosystems, photosynthesis, cellular respiration, human body systems), Earth and space (Earth's layers, rock cycle and plate tectonics, weathering and erosion, weather vs climate, atmosphere, moon phases and tides, solar system), physical science (states of matter, atomic structure, periodic table, chemical vs physical change, energy types and conversions, laws of thermodynamics, waves — sound and light, EM spectrum) [VERIFIED] https://cdn-academy.pressidium.com/academy/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/ASVAB-Checklist.pdf. ASVAB Tutor describes GS as "life, earth, and physical science" with 6 free tests [VERIFIED] https://asvabtutor.com/asvab-practice-tests/. Union Test Prep's GS item 1 is a **Celsius to Fahrenheit** conversion, i.e. UTP treats unit conversion as core GS [VERIFIED] https://uniontestprep.com/asvab/practice-test/general-science/pages/1.

**(b) Must-memorise (cross-guide consensus).**
- Temperature scales: **F = (9/5)C + 32**; 0 C = 32 F, 100 C = 212 F, -40 is the crossover. (Official sample GS item 2 asks 100 C in F; UTP's first GS item is the same concept.)
- Atmosphere: **nitrogen about 78%, oxygen about 21%**, argon about 0.9%, CO2 about 0.04%. (Official sample GS item 4 asks the principal gas.)
- Density and buoyancy reasoning: air is less dense than water **because molecules are farther apart** (official sample GS item 1 — note the trap below).
- Freezing-point depression: salt melts ice by **lowering the freezing point**, not by "chemical destruction" (official sample GS item 3).
- Cell basics: prokaryote vs eukaryote; mitochondria = respiration/ATP; chloroplast = photosynthesis; ribosome = protein synthesis.
- Genetics: dominant/recessive, Punnett squares, genotype vs phenotype, DNA base pairing (A-T, G-C).
- Body systems and the organ-to-function map; blood flow path (right heart to lungs to left heart to body).
- Plate tectonics and rock cycle vocabulary; the three rock classes; weathering vs erosion.
- Energy: kinetic vs potential; conduction/convection/radiation; EM spectrum order (radio, microwave, IR, visible, UV, X-ray, gamma).
- Simple chemistry: atomic number = protons; period = shell; group = valence; pH (below 7 acid, above 7 base); chemical vs physical change.

**(c) Distractor pattern — GS.** *All four options are real science; the difference is one wrong relation.* From the official GS sample:
- "Air is less dense than water because **its molecules are farther apart**" with decoys "it is lighter" (circular, true-sounding), "molecules are closer together" (polarity flip), "it moves more quickly and easily" (a *consequence* dressed as a *cause*). So: **cause/consequence swap plus polarity flip**.
- "100 C = **212 F**" with decoys **32** (the freezing point — the wrong anchor), **100** (the number repeated), **200** (rounding or doubling slip). So: **anchor-value swap**.
- "Principal gas" with argon / neon / oxygen / **nitrogen** — every option really is in air. So: **all-options-are-real-members** (the discriminator is *principal*).
- "Salt melts ice" with decoys "forms an acid", "chemically destroys water molecules", "attracted to concrete". So: **plausible-mechanism fabrication**.

**House rule for authoring GS items:** the stem must contain the discriminating word (*principal*, *primarily*, *most*, *directly*) and every distractor must be a true statement that fails that word. ILLUSTRATIVE (our construction): "Which of these is the most abundant gas in Earth's atmosphere?" (A) oxygen (B) nitrogen (C) argon (D) carbon dioxide — all four real, only one *most*.

**(d) Difficulty.** Guides over-cover here (Mometrix lists thermodynamics and the EM spectrum; Kaplan says "broad outlines" suffice). The real GS is **trivia recall at 48 seconds per item** — 15 items, 12 minutes, no calculation beyond one temperature conversion. Do not build multi-step GS physics problems; the official samples have none.

**(e) Sources (7):** official sample-questions and official GS subtest description; Kaplan technical-subtests article; Dummies GS article; Mometrix 2026 checklist PDF; Union Test Prep GS item set; ASVAB Tutor subtest listing; Achievable content outline.

### 4.2 AR — Arithmetic Reasoning

**(a) Guide coverage.** Kaplan: topics are "number properties, rates, percentages, ratios, proportions, averages, and unit conversions"; "many of the questions will be in the format of word problems"; CAT gives "42 minutes for 15 questions" (wrong, see §7.2) and P&P 36 min for 30 q [VERIFIED] https://www.kaptest.com/study/asvab/asvab-math-strategies/. Kaplan's method is famous and worth copying: **Backsolve** (answers are numeric and "arranged from small to large… start by trying (B) or (C)") and **Pick Numbers** [VERIFIED, same page]. Union Test Prep splits AR into basic properties of numbers, rules of operations, fractions, exponents and percents, then "probability and problem-solving" (mean, median, standard deviation, variance, coefficient of variation, simple and compound interest, d = rt, combined work, arithmetic and geometric sequences) [VERIFIED] https://uniontestprep.com/asvab/resources/important-math-formulas-to-know-for-the-asvab — **wildly over-broad for AR; see §7.4.** Mometrix's maths checklist splits into Number Theory and Operations (PEMDAS, fractions, percent change, ratios, exponents and roots, absolute value, factors and multiples, rounding, squares and cubes, sequences), Data and Probability (mean/median/mode, basic probability, charts, permutations and combinations), Algebra, and Geometry, under the same list for AR and MK [VERIFIED] checklist PDF. Dummies' free AR set is *exactly* real-test shaped: percent increase, probability with a coin bag, unit conversion (pints to gallons), simple interest, 5,280 ft per mile scaling, chart reading, running balance [VERIFIED] https://www.dummies.com/article/academics-the-arts/study-skills-test-prep/armed-services/asvab-arithmetic-reasoning-practice-150180/.

**(b) Must-memorise.**
- **Percent change = (new - old) / old x 100**; discount, markup, tax, tip; "percent of" means multiply by b/100.
- **d = r x t** and its rearrangements; work/rate; combined work **1/t = 1/tA + 1/tB**.
- **Simple interest I = Prt; compound A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)**.
- **Unit-conversion chain**: desired = starting x conversion factor (multiply through, cancel units).
- **English and metric anchors**: 1 mi = 5,280 ft; 1 gal = 4 qt = 8 pt; 1 lb = 16 oz; 1 yd = 3 ft; 1 kg about 2.2 lb; 1 in = 2.54 cm; 1 km about 0.62 mi.
- **Probability = desired / total**; complement = 1 - p.
- Average = sum / count, including "average of a set with a new value" problems.
- Ratio and proportion cross-multiplication; part-to-whole vs part-to-part.
- Time arithmetic (elapsed time, schedules) and money/change arithmetic.
- Order of operations; fraction add/multiply/divide rules.

**(c) Distractor pattern — AR: the unasked intermediate is the distractor.** This is verifiable from the official sample items, item by item:
- *Motorcycle depreciates 12% per year from $7,250* leads to the correct $6,380; the decoy **$870 is the depreciation amount** (the unasked intermediate). [VERIFIED] official AR sample.
- *1 in 9 votes party A; how many vote party B in 810?* leads to 720; the decoy **90 is party A's count** (the other half of the question). [VERIFIED]
- *552 rotations per minute; rotations in half an hour?* leads to 16,560; decoys **276** (halve, wrong direction), **5,520** (x10), **8,280** (x15 — the half-hour-over-2 slip). [VERIFIED]
- *5 seconds for 20 gallons; gallons in 7 seconds?* leads to 28; decoys 24, 26, 30 — adjacent-unit slips. [VERIFIED]
- Dummies' free set repeats the same three devices: the percent-increase item offers **0.5% (25/5000)** — dividing by the wrong base — and **12.5%**; the "1.2 miles in feet" item offers **1,056 ft, which is 0.2 mi in feet** (the unasked intermediate); the coin-bag item offers **1/20, the single-coin probability**; the "left-handed in a class" item offers **4/5, the complement** (the answer to the opposite question); the laundromat item offers **$2.25, one load one way** [VERIFIED] Dummies AR practice page.
- Kaplan's own explanations confirm the same construction and add that **numeric options are sorted ascending**, which is what makes Backsolving work [VERIFIED] Kaplan math strategies page.

**House rule for AR items:** every distractor must be a number a real solver can *arrive at* by a single plausible mis-step: (i) the unasked quantity, (ii) the complement or opposite, (iii) wrong time or size unit, (iv) one step stopped early, (v) a one-percent slip. Never a random number. ILLUSTRATIVE (our construction): "A jacket costs $80 after a 20% discount. What was the original price?" (A) $96 (B) $100 (C) $64 (D) $160 — (A) adds 20% to the sale price (wrong base), (C) applies 20% off twice, (D) halves.

**(d) Difficulty.** AR has the **most generous clock in the battery — 220 seconds per item on CAT**. Guides consistently under-use this: Kaplan's free AR/MK set includes a *find-the-slope-from-a-graph-then-extrapolate* item that is harder than anything in the official samples [VERIFIED] https://www.kaptest.com/study/asvab/asvab-arithmetic-reasoning-and-mathematics-knowledge-practice-questions. Real AR difficulty is **reading comprehension plus one clean arithmetic step**, occasionally two. Build to the official samples, not to Kaplan's free set.

**(e) Sources (7):** official AR sample items; Kaplan math-strategies and AR/MK practice; Dummies AR practice and cheat-sheet counts; Mometrix maths checklist; UTP formula charts; Achievable AR share; ASVAB Tutor AFQT 55-question test.

### 4.3 WK — Word Knowledge

**(a) Guide coverage.** Kaplan publishes a real claim here: **"A little more than half of the questions in the WK section will ask you to define words with no context"**, the rest are in-context; the named sub-skills are **prefix/suffix/root decoding** and **positive/negative/neutral "charge"** [VERIFIED] https://www.kaptest.com/study/asvab/whats-on-the-asvab-word-knowledge/. Kaplan's method: predict the meaning *before* reading options, then eliminate. Dummies' cheat sheet describes WK as "correct meaning of a word; occasionally antonyms (words with opposite meanings)" [VERIFIED] cheat sheet. Mometrix publishes both a skill list (prefix/suffix/root, compound words, synonyms/antonyms, connotation vs denotation, homophones, parts of speech) **and a 31-word vocabulary list** at roughly 11th-grade, technical register — among them *abstain, adhere, bilateral, concave, contingent, deficit, dilate, dilute, discrete, exacerbate, expectorate, febrile, indolent, labile, latent, lethargic, milieu, occlude, overt, paroxysmal, phlegm, potent, rationale, stringent, susceptible, taciturn, tertiary, triage, unseemly, untoward, viscosity* [VERIFIED] Mometrix 2026 checklist PDF. Note the register: **medical and technical Latinate words**, not literary ones. UTP's WK practice is standard synonym format [VERIFIED] uniontestprep.com/asvab. Achievable's outline says WK "covers vocabulary building through prefixes, roots, and suffixes" [VERIFIED] achievable.me.

**(b) Must-memorise — the root and affix table (the highest-ROI memorisation on the whole test).**
- Prefixes: **a-/an-** without; **ab-** away; **ad-** toward; **bene-** good; **circum-** around; **con-/com-** together; **contra-** against; **de-** down/away; **dis-** apart/not; **e-/ex-** out; **in-/im-** not *or* into (two senses); **inter-** between; **intra-** within; **mal-** bad; **mis-** wrong; **ob-** against; **per-** through; **pre-** before; **pro-** forward; **re-** back/again; **sub-** under; **super-** above; **trans-** across; **un-** not.
- Suffixes: **-tion/-sion** noun/act; **-ous/-ious** full of (adjective); **-ity/-ty** state (noun); **-al/-ic** relating to; **-ate** to make; **-able/-ible** capable of; **-ism** doctrine; **-ist** one who; **-ize** to make; **-less** without; **-ment** result.
- Roots: **volu/volv** turn/roll (Kaplan's own worked example: convolution) [VERIFIED] Kaplan; **greg** flock (gregarious); **loqu/locu** speak; **cred** believe; **duc/duct** lead; **fer** carry; **grat** pleasing; **ject** throw; **mit/miss** send; **port** carry; **scrib/script** write; **spec/spect** look; **tract** pull; **vert/vers** turn; **voc** call; **bene/bon** good; **mal** bad; **anthrop** human; **chron** time; **geo** earth; **therm** heat; **phon** sound; **graph** write; **bio** life; **auto** self; **tele** far; **micro** small; **macro** large; **poly** many; **mono** one; **anti** against; **syn/sym** together.
- The **"charge" trick**: if you can only place a word as positive/negative/neutral, Kaplan argues that is often enough to eliminate three options [VERIFIED] Kaplan WK page. This is the single most transferable WK technique in any guide.

**(c) Distractor pattern — WK: morphological neighbours plus the other sense.** From the official WK sample: *antagonize* with decoys *embarrass, struggle, worship* and correct *provoke* — three verbs from the same emotional/social field; *wilted* with *left, budding, requested* and correct *limp* — a near-synonym plus an antonym; *quiver* with *dance, rest, run* and correct *shake* — all motion verbs; *apprehension* in context with *change, enjoyment, endorsement* and correct *anxiety* — where *endorsement* exploits a *different sense* of the word family [VERIFIED] official WK sample page. Kaplan's worked example shows the pure morphological trap: a stem on *anomaly* with options built around *oddity, ally, anonymity* and *heat wave* — two decoys reuse the stem's sound and root [VERIFIED] Kaplan (described, not reproduced).

**House rule for WK items:** the key is a true synonym; the three decoys are (i) a **same-root or same-sound** word, (ii) a **same-domain but wrong-intensity** word, (iii) the **other dictionary sense** of the stem or the antonym. Never a nonsense word — the real test never offers one. ILLUSTRATIVE (our construction): "The supervisor's remarks were **tactful**." (A) tactless (B) discreet (C) tactical (D) touching — (A) antonym, (C) same root, (D) same sound, (B) correct.

**(d) Difficulty.** 36 seconds per item is the tightest clock with the largest knowledge requirement. The **official sample set is easy-to-medium** (antagonize, wilted, quiver); the Mometrix list is materially harder (expectorate, paroxysmal, labile, milieu). Real CAT WK **adapts** — a strong candidate will see words at the Mometrix register. Build both tiers. Kaplan's "a little more than half are no-context" claim should be treated as **[SECONDARY]**: the official description says "words presented in context and… best synonym", and the official samples are three no-context to one in-context, which is consistent with Kaplan's claim but does not prove it — do not hard-code the ratio.

### 4.4 PC — Paragraph Comprehension

**(a) Guide coverage.** Kaplan publishes the most useful numbers in the entire guide market here: **passages are 30–120 words**; on P&P, **15 questions over 13 or 14 passages in 13 minutes**; on CAT, **10 questions in 27 minutes**, and "the extra time allows for the fact that question difficulty can increase as you accumulate correct answers" [VERIFIED] https://www.kaptest.com/study/asvab/whats-on-the-asvab-paragraph-comprehension/. Kaplan names **four question types — Global, Detail, Inference, Vocabulary-in-Context** — and lists the wrong-answer families for each (see (c)). Dummies' cheat sheet says PC passages are "usually a few hundred words" (which **contradicts Kaplan's 30–120 words; see §8**) and gives the practical advice: identify whether the question wants the main point, specific information, or a conclusion, and re-read if unsure [VERIFIED] cheat sheet. Mometrix's PC checklist is the fullest topic inventory: core reading skills (main idea, topic sentence, supporting sentences, summarise, conclusion, author's purpose, tone), critical reading (fact vs opinion, objective vs subjective, evaluate evidence, identify assumptions, compare viewpoints, logical fallacies), interpretation (inference, context clues, conclusions, implied meaning, word meaning, figurative language, author's bias), text structure (cause and effect, sequence, chronological, compare and contrast, problem and solution, transition words) [VERIFIED] checklist PDF. UTP's PC item even ships a hint about qualifier words ("most nearly, most likely, usually, probably") [VERIFIED] https://uniontestprep.com/asvab/practice-test/paragraph-comprehension/pages/1. Achievable describes PC as "obtain information from written passages as well as interpretive skill" [VERIFIED].

**(b) Must-memorise.** There is almost nothing to memorise here — PC is a **procedure**, and every guide converges on it: read the question first, know which of the four types it is, locate or paraphrase, predict, eliminate. What is worth memorising:
- The **four question types and their stems**: Global (*main idea, purpose, tone, primarily concerned with*), Detail (*according to the passage…*), Inference (*implies, can be inferred, apparently feels*), Vocabulary-in-Context (*as used in the passage, X most nearly means*).
- **Contrast and conclusion signposts**: *but, though, however, although, yet* (the clause after the contrast word usually carries the author's view — Kaplan [VERIFIED]); *thus, therefore, I suggest, I believe* (conclusion markers).
- **Qualifier vocabulary**: *most nearly, most likely, usually, probably* means pick the best answer, not the only true one (UTP hint [VERIFIED]).
- Nothing outside the passage is ever required [VERIFIED] Kaplan ("no outside knowledge is required").

**(c) Distractor pattern — PC: four named trap families.** Kaplan publishes them explicitly, and they hold up against the official items:
- **Global:** too specific, too general, contradicts, too extreme [VERIFIED] Kaplan PC page.
- **Detail:** text lifted from an **irrelevant part of the passage** — "recognizing text from the passage" is not the same as "text that answers the question" [VERIFIED] Kaplan.
- **Inference:** contradicts, outside information, distorts, extreme claim [VERIFIED] Kaplan.
- **Official confirmation:** the titanium passage asks *why artists work with titanium*; the decoys are "is transparent" (an attribute of the oxide, not the metal), **"does not corrode"** (explicitly true in the passage but *not* the reason artists use it), and "generates its own heat" (distortion) [VERIFIED] official PC sample. That is the **true-but-not-the-answer** trap Kaplan warns about, appearing in an official item.
- The east-Texas passage's inference item ("does NOT resemble…") uses the **negation stem** plus the "boots-and-saddle image… sand and cacti" cue, yielding *desert*; decoys are other landscape categories [VERIFIED] official PC sample.

**House rule for PC items:** each passage should be 30–120 words (per Kaplan) with one unambiguous correct paraphrase and three named traps; **include at least one "true in the passage but not the answer" distractor**, because the real test does. ILLUSTRATIVE (our construction): a 60-word passage on a bridge's expansion joints, followed by "According to the passage, the gaps in the roadway are included mainly to…" with one correct paraphrase, one detail lifted from the paragraph about paint, one over-generalisation, and one true-but-unasked fact.

**(d) Difficulty.** PC is the **slowest** subtest per item after AR (162 seconds per item) and the passages are short — difficulty comes from trap density, not passage length. Do **not** write 300-word passages.

**(e) Sources (6):** official PC sample items; Kaplan PC article; Dummies cheat sheet; Mometrix PC checklist; UTP PC item set and hint; Achievable outline.

### 4.5 MK — Mathematics Knowledge

**(a) Guide coverage.** Kaplan: "applied arithmetic, algebra, and geometry"; "the occasional word problem… but in general the questions are more direct than AR"; CAT "23 minutes for 15 questions" (wrong — official is 31, and 23 was the retired 2020 figure, see §7.2) [VERIFIED] https://www.kaptest.com/study/asvab/asvab-math-strategies/. Mometrix's Algebra and Geometry block is the cleanest published scope: linear equations and inequalities, systems, polynomials, factoring, quadratics, functions and relations, word problems with variables; perimeter and area, volume and surface area, Pythagorean theorem, coordinate geometry basics; plus applied math (simple and compound interest, profit/loss/discount, unit conversion) [VERIFIED] checklist PDF. Dummies' MK piece teaches **3-D volume** explicitly — "questions involving three-dimensional figures may jump off the screen at any time… memorizing the volume formulas for the most common 3D figures, like spheres, cubes, and pyramids" [VERIFIED] https://www.dummies.com/article/asvab-mathematics-knowledge-practice-volume-242226. UTP's MK chart adds algebra manipulation, **special products (a plus-minus b) squared, difference and sum of squares and cubes**, quadratics, and linear equations Ax + By = C [VERIFIED] UTP formulas page. Kaplan adds the strategy layer (Backsolve, Pick Numbers) shared with AR.

**(b) Must-memorise — the definitive MK formula card.**
- Perimeter and area: circle **A = pi r squared, C = 2 pi r**; triangle **A = half b h**; rectangle **A = l w**; parallelogram **A = b h**; trapezoid **A = half (b1 + b2) h**.
- Volume: prism or box **V = l w h**; cylinder **V = pi r squared h**; pyramid or cone **V = one third of base area x h**; sphere **V = four thirds pi r cubed**; cube **V = s cubed**.
- Surface area: box **SA = 2(lw + lh + wh)**; cylinder **SA = 2 pi r squared + 2 pi r h**; sphere **SA = 4 pi r squared**.
- Right triangles: **a squared + b squared = c squared**; triples **3-4-5, 5-12-13, 8-15-17**; **45-45-90 gives x, x, x root 2**; **30-60-90 gives x, x root 3, 2x**.
- Exponent rules: **x^a times x^b = x^(a+b); x^a / x^b = x^(a-b); (x^a)^b = x^(ab); (xy)^a = x^a y^a; x^0 = 1; x^(-a) = 1/x^a; x^(a/b) = b-th root of x^a**.
- Algebra identities: **(a plus-minus b) squared = a squared plus-minus 2ab + b squared; a squared - b squared = (a-b)(a+b); a cubed plus-minus b cubed factorisations** (UTP).
- Solving: isolate the variable; from **x/a = b** get **x = ab**; inequalities flip when you multiply or divide by a negative.
- Percentage, interest and ratio (shared with AR).
- Mean, median, mode; simple probability (Mometrix puts these under MK too).
- Coordinate geometry: **slope m = (y2 - y1)/(x2 - x1)**; **y = m x + b**; distance = square root of ((x2-x1) squared + (y2-y1) squared); midpoint.

**(c) Distractor pattern — MK: the wrong quantity, and the factor-of-three slip.**
- Official MK brick item: options 15 / 36 / 44 / **96** — the decoys are **perimeter- and surface-area-shaped numbers** for a small rectangular solid, i.e. *the wrong quantity*. [VERIFIED] official MK sample.
- Official **ratio 36:12** item: 2:1 / **3:1** / 4:1 / 5:1 — off-by-one on the divisor. [VERIFIED]
- Official **(x squared - y squared)/(x - y)** item: **x + y** with decoys **x - y, x + 2y, 2x - y** — sign and coefficient slips on the factored form. [VERIFIED]
- Dummies' **pyramid volume** item: 450 / **500** / 1,500 / 150 — the decoys cluster around *forgetting the one-third* (1,500) and *halving it twice* (150). [VERIFIED] Dummies MK practice page.
- Kaplan's free MK item (LCM of three numbers) offers **one of the inputs** and **the product of two of the inputs** as decoys [VERIFIED] Kaplan AR/MK practice page (described, not reproduced).

**House rule for MK items:** at least two distractors must be **quantities the stem could plausibly have asked for** (area when volume is asked, perimeter when area is asked, the depreciation amount when the value is asked) and one must be the **symbolic slip** (sign, coefficient, dropped one-third, dropped square). ILLUSTRATIVE (our construction): "A right circular cylinder has r = 3 and h = 5. Its volume is…" (A) 15 pi (dropped r, used pi r h), (B) 30 pi (diameter slip), (C) 45 pi (correct), (D) 90 pi (used d instead of r).

**(d) Difficulty.** 124 seconds per item — MK is *fast* compared with AR because the items are direct. No calculator is permitted anywhere in the battery [VERIFIED] official what-to-expect ("You cannot use a calculator"). Guides note MK items are "more direct than the word problems on AR" (Kaplan) — build mostly one-concept items with occasional two-step algebra.

### 4.6 EI — Electronics Information

**(a) Guide coverage.** Kaplan: "basic principles of electricity and electronic components… identify major circuit components and their functions, **perform calculations to determine circuit properties**, and understand some of the safety devices used in modern household wiring" [VERIFIED] Kaplan technical-subtests page. Dummies' EI page is the most concrete: a units table, Ohm's law, power, current direction, closed circuit, AC and DC, frequency in hertz, reactance, rectifier, transistor [VERIFIED] https://www.dummies.com/article/academics-the-arts/study-skills-test-prep/armed-services/helpful-tips-for-the-asvab-electronic-information-test-256918/. Mometrix's EI checklist: charge and current, voltage, resistance, **Ohm's law V = IR**, **power P = VI**, series and parallel, AC vs DC, frequency and period, Kirchhoff's voltage and current laws; components — resistors and colour codes, capacitors, inductors, diodes, transistors (NPN/PNP), transformers, fuses and breakers, switches and relays; systems — semiconductors, integrated circuits, grounding, units, generators and motors, wiring and insulation types [VERIFIED] checklist PDF. UTP has a full EI set [VERIFIED]. ASVAB Tutor: "Electrical principles and circuits" [VERIFIED].

**(b) Must-memorise — the EI core.**
- **Units:** current = ampere (A); voltage = volt (V); resistance = ohm; power = watt (W); energy = watt-hour; frequency = hertz (Hz). (Dummies' own list [VERIFIED].)
- **Ohm's law V = IR** (Dummies prints it as **I = V/R**; same law) and **P = VI** (Dummies prints **P = EI**, using E for voltage — an important notation trap, see §8), plus the derived forms **P = I squared R** and **P = V squared / R**.
- **Series:** one path; **current is the same everywhere**; **R total = R1 + R2 + …**; voltages add. **Parallel:** **voltage is the same across each branch**; **1/R total = 1/R1 + 1/R2 + …**; currents add.
- **Conductors and insulators**; the standard conductor ranking (silver, copper, gold, aluminium); **resistance rises with length and temperature, falls with thickness**.
- **Component functions:** resistor limits current; capacitor stores charge (blocks DC, passes AC); inductor opposes change in current (magnetic field); diode allows one-way current (a rectifier converts AC to DC); transistor amplifies or switches; transformer changes AC voltage (not DC); fuse or breaker opens on overload; relay is a switch operated by a coil; **ground** is a safety return path.
- **AC vs DC:** AC reverses direction periodically; DC flows one way; frequency is cycles per second.
- **Safety:** fuse in the hot leg, grounding, insulation, never overload a circuit, water plus electricity.

**(c) Distractor pattern — EI: other-quantity labels, initials that fit, and tube-era folklore.** From the official EI sample:
- *"Which of the following is measured using an ohmmeter?"* leads to **resistance**; decoys **voltage, inductance, capacitance** — all four are real electrical quantities, three of which have their own meters. So: **sibling-quantity decoys**. [VERIFIED]
- *"What does AC stand for?"* leads to **alternating current**; decoys *additional charge, alternating coil, ampere current* — every decoy expands the same two initials. So: **initialism decoys**. [VERIFIED]
- *"Because solid-state diodes have no filament, they…"* leads to *require less operating power*; decoys *don't work, are less efficient than tubes, require more operating power* — a **less/more polarity pair** plus the **obsolete-technology comparison**. [VERIFIED]
- *"The current in the 2-ohm resistor is…"* with options one-third, one-half, 1 and 3 amperes — **reciprocal and fraction slips** around V/R. [VERIFIED]

**House rule for EI items:** options should be (i) a sibling quantity, (ii) a same-initials expansion or same-family component name, (iii) a reversed-polarity statement, (iv) the reciprocal of the right number. **The official EI sample includes a circuit-diagram item** — EI is a diagram subtest, not just recall.

**(d) Difficulty.** 40 seconds per item. Real EI is **recall plus one-step Ohm or power arithmetic plus component identification from a symbol or photo**; it is not Kirchhoff's laws. Mometrix's inclusion of Kirchhoff and resistor colour codes is over-preparation for the real test but harmless.

**(e) Sources (6):** official EI sample items; Dummies EI article; Kaplan technical-subtests; Mometrix EI checklist; UTP EI set; ASVAB Tutor listing.

### 4.9 MC — Mechanical Comprehension

**(a) Guide coverage.** Kaplan: "applied physics… practical application of the laws of physics"; **22 minutes for 15 items on CAT**, 19 minutes for 25 on P&P [VERIFIED] Kaplan technical-subtests. Dummies has whole articles on MC mechanics — including a full explanation of **work as force over a distance, measured in foot-pounds** ("one foot-pound of work occurs when a 1-pound weight is lifted to a height of 1 foot"), the distinction between **effort** and **work**, and the point that resistance is not the same as weight (friction on a floor vs lifting) [VERIFIED] https://www.dummies.com/article/armed-services-33776/asvab-mechanical-comprehension-subtest-determining-work-142340. Dummies' MC tips page adds the meta-strategy: MC items "often include illustrations… parts of the device are labeled. Make certain you read and understand these labels" [VERIFIED] https://www.dummies.com/article/academics-the-arts/study-skills-test-prep/armed-services/tips-for-a-better-test-score-on-the-asvab-mechanical-comprehension-subtest-142350/. Mometrix's MC checklist is the most complete published scope: Newton's laws, F = ma, gravity, static and kinetic friction, work/power/energy, kinetic and potential energy, conservation, momentum and impulse, torque and rotation, centripetal force; simple machines — levers (1st, 2nd and 3rd class), pulleys, inclined planes, wedges, screws and threads, wheel and axle, gears, cams and cranks, mechanical advantage; fluid mechanics — liquid pressure, **Pascal, Boyle, Archimedes, Bernoulli**; structures — tension and compression, beams/supports/loads, stability, **Hooke's law F = kx**, heat transfer [VERIFIED] checklist PDF. UTP and ASVAB Tutor cover MC as "forces, motion, and simple machines" [VERIFIED].

**(b) Must-memorise — MC core.**
- **W = F x d** (work = force times distance) in **foot-pounds** (US) or joules; **Power = work / time**.
- **Mechanical advantage:** lever **MA = effort arm / resistance arm**; **1st class** (fulcrum between), **2nd class** (resistance between, MA greater than 1), **3rd class** (effort between, MA less than 1, a speed multiplier); pulley systems: **MA = number of rope strands supporting the load**; inclined plane **MA = length / height**; wheel and axle **MA = R / r**.
- **Gears:** ratio = teeth ratio; **touching gears turn in opposite directions**; a larger driven gear turns slower with more torque.
- **Pressure = force / area**; pressure in a fluid acts **equally in all directions** at a given depth; **hydraulic MA = area ratio** (Pascal).
- **Density and buoyancy:** an object floats if its density is less than the fluid's; **Archimedes: buoyant force = weight of displaced fluid**.
- **Friction:** opposes motion; kinetic is less than static; depends on surfaces and normal force, not on contact area.
- **Energy:** KE = one half m v squared; PE = m g h; energy is conserved but not used up.
- **Torque = force x distance from the pivot**; **centre of gravity** — lowering it or widening the base increases stability; extending a crane's reach **shifts its centre of gravity** (official MC sample [VERIFIED]).
- **Heat transfer:** conduction (solids, contact), convection (fluids), radiation (no medium).
- **Vectors:** forces combine; equilibrium when net force = 0.

**(c) Distractor pattern — MC: reversed-inequality pairs and sibling technical terms.** From the official MC sample:
- *"Why does the intake valve open when the piston goes down?"* leads to *air pressure at X is less than at Y*; the decoys include **the exact reversal** (*X greater than Y*) and **third-point comparisons** (*Z less than X*, *Y greater than Z*). So: **the mirrored inequality is always present.** [VERIFIED]
- *"Extending the reach of this crane will shift its…"* leads to **centre of gravity**; the decoy is **centre of buoyancy**, a real physics term one word away. [VERIFIED]
- *"What type of gear arrangement is this?"* offers **spur / bevel / rack-and-pinion / worm-and-wheel** — all real gear types, discriminated by a picture. [VERIFIED]
- *"In which direction does friction act on this skier?"* and *"Which shelf will support the most weight?"* are A/B/C/D diagram items. So: **the diagram is the item; the options are the same diagram with one changed vector or support.** [VERIFIED]

**House rule for MC items:** for every quantitative claim, include **both directions** of the comparison among the options; for every device, use **four real sibling devices**; for every diagram, all four options must be the *same* diagram with exactly one property changed. ILLUSTRATIVE (our construction): "Two identical boxes slide across the same floor; box A is pushed with 50 N and box B with 80 N. Which statement is true?" (A) A needs more force to keep moving (B) B experiences greater friction (C) both experience equal friction (D) friction is zero — (B) is the intended answer and (A)/(C) are the reversed and equal variants.

**(d) Difficulty.** 88 seconds per item with diagrams. Real MC is **one principle per item**, diagram-driven, with no multi-step computation; the hardest official sample is the pump-valve pressure comparison. Guides overshoot (Bernoulli, Hooke, momentum, centripetal force) — useful for edge cases, not the core.

**(e) Sources (6):** official MC sample items; Dummies MC tips and work article; Kaplan technical-subtests; Mometrix MC checklist; UTP MC set; ASVAB Tutor.

### 4.10 AO — Assembling Objects

**(a) Guide coverage.** Kaplan treats AO as spatial aptitude and describes it alongside "jigsaw problems" [VERIFIED] Kaplan technical-subtests. Dummies is again the most concrete: it names the **two item families** — **connection-type problems** and **fit-together / jigsaw** — and gives the elimination procedure (isolate one shape or one **dot**, scan all four choices for that feature in a different orientation) plus the explicit warning that "the sneaky test-makers often make use of such **mirror representations**" [VERIFIED] https://www.dummies.com/article/academics-the-arts/study-skills-test-prep/armed-services/tips-for-getting-a-better-score-on-the-asvab-assembling-objects-subtest-142277/ and https://www.dummies.com/article/asvab-assembling-objects-subtest-shapes-142267 (note the article's own copy admits its worked examples are "warm-up exercises — the questions on the ASVAB are harder"). Mometrix's AO checklist is unusually well organised for a spatial subtest: mental rotation of 2D and 3D shapes, spatial visualisation, figure-ground, part-whole, mirror images and reflections, congruent shapes, pattern recognition; then part orientation (track edge direction, match angle orientation, notice rotation vs flip, compare attachment positions, check side-to-side, top/bottom orientation); shape relationships (match touching edges, identify connection points, relative part size, corner and side placement, **distinguish similar-looking distractors**, spot missing or extra features, overlapping features, interior vs exterior lines); final assembly (predict the outline, recognise after rotation, recognise after reflection, check all pieces accounted for) [VERIFIED] checklist PDF.

**(b) Must-memorise.** AO has no facts to memorise — it has **rules of procedure**:
- Identify the **type**: *connection* (two labelled parts joined by a line between dots) or *fit-together* (jigsaw of two pieces).
- **Rotation is legal; reflection is not.** A mirrored option is always wrong (Dummies names this the primary trap).
- Check **one feature at a time** across all four options (a single dot's position, a single edge's direction).
- For connection items: the **dot's position** on each part, and **where the line enters or exits** the shape, are the discriminating features.
- For jigsaw items: **count edges and outline**, and check that no option *adds* or *removes* a notch.
- Options show the **same parts rearranged** — if an option contains a part you do not have, eliminate on sight.

**(c) Distractor pattern — AO: the mirror image, plus similar-looking variants.** The official AO sample shows the two stems verbatim: *"Which figure best shows how the objects in the left box will touch if the letters for each object are matched?"* and *"Which figure best shows how the objects in the left box will appear if they are fit together?"* — the options are labelled A to D figures [VERIFIED] official AO sample page. Guides agree the decoys are (i) a **reflection** of the correct assembly, (ii) the correct assembly **rotated with one part mis-rotated**, (iii) a **near-touching** variant (the dot or edge is adjacent, not attached) [VERIFIED] Dummies AO tips plus Mometrix AO checklist ("distinguish similar-looking distractors", "recognize completed shape after reflection"). **House rule:** for every AO item, one option must be the mirror image, one must have the correct parts in the wrong relative orientation, and one must show the parts close but not touching.

**(d) Difficulty.** 72 seconds per item, visual only. AO is **not an AFQT subtest** [VERIFIED] honestmos plus the official AFQT definition — an important triage fact for candidates short on time. Guides note it is often the *easiest* subtest for mechanically minded candidates and the most trainable with practice.

**(e) Sources (6):** official AO sample items; Dummies AO tips, shapes and connectors articles; Kaplan AO practice page; Mometrix AO checklist; UTP AO bank; ASVAB Tutor AO (3 tests).

## 5. Distractor design, summarised for the item writer

| Subtest | Primary decoy device | Secondary device | Verified from |
|---|---|---|---|
| **GS** | Cause/consequence swap; polarity flip ("farther apart" vs "closer together") | Wrong anchor value (32 F vs 212 F); all options are real members of the set | Official GS sample |
| **AR** | **The unasked intermediate** (the depreciation amount, the other party's count, the 0.2-mile portion) | Complement or opposite answer; wrong time or size unit; one step stopped early | Official AR sample plus Dummies AR set |
| **WK** | Same-root or same-sound neighbour | Wrong intensity in the same domain; the word's other sense; the antonym | Official WK sample plus Kaplan |
| **PC** | True-in-passage-but-not-the-answer | Too specific / too general / too extreme / contradicts | Kaplan's four families plus official PC sample |
| **MK** | **Wrong quantity** (area vs volume, perimeter vs area) | Dropped one-third or dropped square; sign or coefficient slip; off-by-one ratio | Official MK sample plus Dummies pyramid item |
| **EI** | **Sibling quantity** (voltage/inductance/capacitance for an ohmmeter) | Initialism that fits; less/more polarity pair; reciprocal of the right number | Official EI sample |
| **AI** | Wrong system's component; adjacent stroke | Reversed cause and effect; real part, wrong function | [SECONDARY] guides only — validate |
| **SI** | **Four real tools from one family**, with the stem supplying the one discriminating attribute | Plausible-purpose fabrication (sanding block) | Official SI sample |
| **MC** | **The mirrored inequality is always an option** | Sibling technical term (centre of gravity vs buoyancy); same diagram, one vector changed | Official MC sample |
| **AO** | **Mirror image** (reflection, not rotation) | Correct parts, wrong relative orientation; near-touching but not attached | Official AO sample plus Dummies and Mometrix |

**Cross-cutting laws the real test follows, which guides' items often break:**
1. **Four options, always** — every official sample item has exactly four.
2. **No "none of the above", no "all of the above", no joke options.** I found none in any official sample.
3. **No negative stems except in PC** — the official PC sample has a "does NOT resemble" item; the maths and technical samples have none.
4. **Every distractor is a real, reachable answer** — decoys are things a solver can actually compute or genuinely recall. Random or nonsense options are a guide tell, and a bad one.
5. **The stem carries the discriminator** (*principal*, *most nearly*, *best*, *most*), and the distractors are true statements that fail that word.

**What we can say about difficulty, with evidence:**

1. **Paper practice sets skew EASIER than the current CAT for the maths subtests.** Dummies' free AR set is textbook easy-to-medium: simple interest on $2,000 at 4%, pints in 2 gallons, running-balance arithmetic [VERIFIED] Dummies AR practice. Official AR is comparable. So Dummies' AR is well calibrated.
2. **Kaplan's free question sets skew HARDER than the real test.** Its AR/MK set includes a slope-from-a-graph-then-extrapolate item and an abstract "which equation is a straight line" item [VERIFIED] Kaplan AR/MK practice. Neither has a counterpart in the official samples.
3. **Mometrix openly admits the skew**: "Because of how thorough the guide is, **it can seem more difficult than the test itself**" [VERIFIED] mometrix.com/asvab-study-guide.
4. **The real test has no fixed difficulty mix** — the CAT branches to an easier item after a wrong answer and a harder one after a right answer [VERIFIED] official fact sheet, "How the CAT-ASVAB Works". So "how many easy/medium/hard" is **a property of the candidate, not the form**: a 55th-percentile candidate and a 90th-percentile candidate sit different tests.
5. **A third vendor's critique of books** (useful as a secondary check): "A book gives you two or three full practice tests and no way to see your trend line by subtest… **Answer keys over explanations**. Most book answers are a letter and a sentence." [SECONDARY] https://asvabdrill.com/posts/asvab-for-dummies

**Our authoring recommendation** (explicitly **our own design target, not a published fact** — [UNVERIFIED] as published data): because the real CAT serves roughly half of its items above the candidate's current ability estimate, an app that wants to feel real should present **about 30% easy, 50% medium, 20% hard within each practice set**, and should re-serve harder items after correct streaks. Per-subtest tuning: AR and MK need the largest hard share (30%) because guides underserve hard AR; SI, AI and EI need the largest easy share (breadth of recall matters more than depth); WK needs a hard tail (the real CAT will feed strong candidates words at the expectorate/paroxysmal register).

## 7. What guides get WRONG or teach that is OUT OF DATE — the do-not-do-this list

This is the most actionable section for the app. Each item is a concrete, sourced defect.

### 7.1 Live "ASVAB For Dummies" web content still teaches pre-2020 CAT item counts (16 questions)

The Dummies article **"Breakdown of the ASVAB Subtests"** (by Rod Powers, **updated 2016-07-24**, still live and top-ranked) publishes: GS **16 q / 8 min**, AR **16 q / 39 min**, WK **16 q / 8 min**, PC **11 q / 22 min**, MK **16 q / 20 min**, EI **16 q / 8 min**, AI **11 q / 7 min**, SI **11 q / 6 min**, MC **16 q / 20 min**, AO **16 q / 15 min**. Every one of those counts and times is **wrong for 2024–2026** (official: 15/12, 15/55, 15/9, 10/27, 15/31, 15/10, 10/7, 10/6, 15/22, 15/18). It also claims a **Coding Speed** subtest exists "for a few Navy jobs". [VERIFIED] https://www.dummies.com/article/academics-the-arts/study-skills-test-prep/armed-services/breakdown-of-the-asvab-subtests-169176/ versus [VERIFIED] official 2026 fact sheet.
So: **do not** publish 16-item counts, and **do not** reference Coding Speed as a live subtest.

### 7.2 Kaplan's own study pages publish wrong AR and MK times — and contradict Kaplan's other page

- Kaplan math-strategies page: AR "**42 minutes** to answer 15 questions"; MK "**23 minutes** to answer 15 questions" [VERIFIED] kaptest.com/study/asvab/asvab-math-strategies.
- Official: **AR 55 minutes, MK 31 minutes** [VERIFIED] 2026 fact sheet.
- Kaplan's *technical* page on the same site gets every *other* subtest right and matches the official table [VERIFIED] kaptest.com/study/asvab/whats-on-the-asvab-technical-subtests.
- The **MK 23 minutes is the retired 2020 official figure** (see §2.3) — clear evidence of a page frozen at a 2020 snapshot. The AR 42 minutes matches no official printing I can find, so it is an arithmetic or typographical error carried forward. [DERIVED]
So: **do not** copy Kaplan's timing table wholesale; the AR and MK cells are wrong *inside* a page that is otherwise right.

### 7.3 Dummies says guessing is nearly free on the CAT; the official document says the opposite

Dummies: "The ASVAB doesn't penalize you for guessing (unless you're making a lot of guesses at the end of a computerized subtest)"; and elsewhere in the same article, "If you leave a question blank, you have a zero percent chance of getting it right, but if you guess, you have at least a 25 percent chance" [VERIFIED] Dummies cheat sheet. The official 2026 fact sheet: "**the CAT-ASVAB has a penalty for guessing**" and advises against random guessing at the end [VERIFIED]. Dummies' framing is defensible for *elimination-based* guessing (and its 25/33/50 arithmetic is fine), but the app must not tell candidates "guessing is free on the computer test".

### 7.4 Free-site formula sheets are not ASVAB-scoped

Union Test Prep's ASVAB formula page includes, for AR and MK, standard deviation **s = root of (sum of (x - mean) squared / (n - 1))**, variance, coefficient of variation, conditional probability P(A given B), mutually exclusive events, arithmetic and geometric sequence formulas, logarithms, cube factorisations, and compound interest with n periods [VERIFIED] https://uniontestprep.com/asvab/resources/important-math-formulas-to-know-for-the-asvab. None of that appears in any official sample item, and no guide's own scope list includes standard deviation, coefficient of variation or logarithms. UTP also warns you must memorise them because "you won't have access to these formulas during the test" — true, but scaring candidates into memorising sigma is a **time-allocation error**. [DERIVED]
So: **do not** copy UTP's chart. Use the MK card in §4.5.

### 7.5 Peterson's current *print* guide is from 2018

Master the ASVAB, 23rd edition, 608 pages, **publish date May 2018** [VERIFIED] stevensbooks product details. A 2018 prep book was printed under the **pre-2020 CAT time limits** (GS 10 min, MK 23 min, AO 17 min — §2.3) and possibly 16-item counts. Peterson's has clearly moved its product to the online course ("Updated for 2026", 1,627 questions) [VERIFIED] petersons.com — but any app or article that cites "Peterson's Master the ASVAB" as current is citing a 2018 artefact.
So: **do not** treat Peterson's print as a 2024–2026 source.

### 7.6 McGraw-Hill appears to have no 2024–2026 ASVAB edition

The only McGraw-Hill ASVAB product I could locate is the Waller **2023** edition (ISBN 978-1-264-27746-9) [SECONDARY — the product page returned only site chrome to my scraper; the edition year comes from the URL slug]. **I could not verify any 2024–2026 McGraw-Hill ASVAB printing.** [UNVERIFIED that one exists]
So: treat McGraw-Hill as a 2023 source at newest, and verify any page number before citing it.

### 7.7 The famous NO/CS line-score trap is real, and guides and sites still fall into it

Older composite and line-score formulas used **Numerical Operations (NO)** and **Coding Speed (CS)** — two subtests that **no longer exist on the CAT-ASVAB**. A 2026 line-score explainer states the warning plainly: "Older composite formulas used two subtests that no longer exist on the computer-adaptive ASVAB — Numerical Operations (NO) and Coding Speed (CS). If you find a formula online that includes NO or CS, it's from a legacy version." [SECONDARY] https://www.honestmos.com/tools/asvab-line-scores. Dummies' 2016 article still names Coding Speed as live [VERIFIED] (§7.1). **The only composite formula both that page and the guides will stand behind is GT = VE + AR** (VE = WK + PC) [SECONDARY, consistent with the official AFQT definition 2VE + AR + MK].
So: **do not** print NO/CS, and do not print Army or Navy composite formulas without a current official source.

### 7.8 A guide's own practice-test arithmetic is inconsistent

Barron's 14th-edition blurb: "**7 full-length practice tests — 4 in the book and 4 online**" [VERIFIED] simonandschuster.com. Four plus four is eight, not seven. This is cosmetic, but it is a reminder that vendor marketing copy is not audited. If the app quotes a guide's test count, quote it as *the publisher's claim*.

### 7.9 Guides publish contradictory facts about passage length

- **Dummies**: PC "questions based on several paragraphs (**usually a few hundred words**)" [VERIFIED] cheat sheet.
- **Kaplan**: passages are **30–120 words**; P&P has 15 questions over 13–14 passages [VERIFIED] Kaplan PC page.
- **Official**: "obtain information from written passages" — no length given [VERIFIED] official what-to-expect.
Kaplan's range is far more plausible for a 162-second item, and it matches the official sample passages (two to four sentences). [DERIVED] So build 30–120-word passages.

### 7.10 Achievable's AFQT percentages describe item share, not score share — and its format summary is wrong

Achievable publishes: AR "**27% – 29%**", WK "**27% – 33%**", PC "**14% – 18%**", MK "**24% – 27%**" [VERIFIED] https://achievable.me/exams/asvab/exam-info/. Those ranges are the **share of AFQT questions** (15/55 = 27.3%; 10/55 = 18.2%), not the share of the **score**, because VE is **doubled**: verbal is about 55.6% of AFQT raw weight, AR and MK about 16.7% each (§3). The same page also states "**135 – 150 questions (CAT-ASVAB)**" and "**3 hours 17+ minutes**", both of which overstate the official 135 items and 197 minutes [VERIFIED versus official fact sheet]. It also lists AFQT minimums (Army 31, USMC 31, Navy 35, Air Force 31, Coast Guard 32) [VERIFIED] which match the commonly published 2024–2026 values [SECONDARY].
So: **do not** present "AR is 28% of the ASVAB" to a candidate. It is 11.1% of items and 16.7% of AFQT raw score.

### 7.11 Mometrix's free checklists overshoot the tested scope (harmlessly, but expensively)

Included on the **Electrical** checklist: Kirchhoff's voltage and current laws, resistor colour codes, NPN/PNP transistor detail, reactance, wiring and insulation types. On **Mechanical**: Bernoulli's principle, Hooke's law, momentum and impulse, centripetal force. On **Math**: permutations and combinations [VERIFIED] Mometrix 2026 checklist PDF. None of these appear in official samples; several are a stretch for a 40-second item. The checklist itself disclaims completeness ("this checklist does not cover everything that's on the ASVAB") but not *over*-coverage. [DERIVED]
So: use Mometrix's **topic inventory** as a safety net, not as a priority list.

### 7.12 Study.com cannot be verified from this environment

Every attempt to fetch study.com/asvab/* returned a CAPTCHA page [VERIFIED that it was blocked]. Any claim attributed to Study.com in app copy must be flagged **[UNVERIFIED]** until a human reads the page.

## 11. What the app should copy, and what it must not

**Copy:**
- The real distractor devices in §5 — they are the single most transferable finding in this study, and they are documented in official items, not just in guides.
- Kaplan's method framing: predict-then-eliminate for WK and PC; **Backsolve** and **Pick Numbers** for AR and MK (and teach that numeric options are sorted ascending, which makes Backsolve work).
- Dummies' AO procedure (one dot or one edge across all four options; reflection is always wrong) and Mometrix's AO checklist as a debugging rubric.
- The honest arithmetic in §3: verbal is 55.6% of AFQT raw weight; AR is 11.1% of items, not 28%.
- The tryout-item table (§2.2) — genuinely useful, and almost nobody publishes it.

**Do not copy:**
- Any 16-item CAT count, any AR or MK time other than 55 and 31, any reference to Coding Speed or NO as live subtests (§7.1, §7.2, §7.7).
- UTP's statistics and logarithm formula sheet (§7.4), or Mometrix's graduate-level technical topics (§7.11), as a priority list.
- "Guessing is free on the computer test" (§7.3).
- "AR is 27–29% of the ASVAB" without saying *of what* (§7.10).
- "Peterson's Master the ASVAB" as a current edition (§7.5), or "Study.com says…" as verified (§7.12).
