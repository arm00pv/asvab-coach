ractice Test Before creating your study schedule, take an ASVAB practice test to establish a starting point. You don't need to wait until you have reviewed every topic. At this stage, the go
al is to identify where your preparation time will have the greatest effect. Review your results by subtest: Arithmetic Reasoning: Can you translate word problems into the correct calculatio
ns and work accurately with fractions, percentages, ratios, rates, and proportions? Mathematics Knowledge: Do you need more practice with algebra, equations, exponents, or geometry? Word Kno
wledge/Paragraph Comprehension: Can you determine word meanings from context, identify synonyms, locate important details, and understand the main point of a passage? General Science: Do you
 need to review temperature conversions, chemical elements, or molecular structure? Electronics, Auto, Shop, and Mechanical Information: Are you familiar with electrical principles, tools, a
utomotive systems, and basic mechanical concepts? Assembling Objects: Can you determine how separate pieces will fit together or how an object will look once assembled? Identifying those spe
cific weaknesses will show you where to devote more attention during your study time. Review Difficult Questions After each practice session, review every question you answered incorrectly A
ND every question you guessed on or answered with low confidence. Guessing correctly does not necessarily mean that you have mastered the skill. For each difficult question, determine which 
of the following caused the problem: You did not know the topic or concept. You knew the required math but could not translate the word problem. You overlooked a detail or context clue. You 
confused two technical terms or scientific principles. You had trouble visualizing how the pieces of an object fit together. You made a calculation error while working without a calculator. 
You spent too long trying to determine the answer. You selected an answer because it looked familiar without verifying that it was correct. Then, outline a specific plan for improvement base
d on what you discovered. In other words, don't just say, “I need to study math more.” Note that you need to practice solving rate problems without a calculator or reviewing how to calculate
 the area and volume of common shapes. Similarly, replace “study mechanical concepts” with a specific task such as reviewing gear direction, mechanical advantage, or the effects of friction.
 Utilize Answer Explanations Answer explanations are an underutilized studying resource. To take full advantage of them, read the explanation for every question you found difficult, includin
g questions you answered correctly. For each one, make sure you can confidently explain: Why the correct answer is correct Why each incorrect option is wrong What rule, concept, or skill is 
being tested How the same skill might appear in a different question For math questions, be sure to complete every calculation by hand, since calculators are not allowed on the ASVAB. For sc
ience and technical questions, explain the relevant process or principle in your own words. Study Concepts, Not Individual Questions Memorizing the answer to an ASVAB practice question will 
not prepare you for a new question testing the same skill with a different passage, equation, graph, or set of numbers. Your goal should be to recognize and apply the underlying skill to any
 question that asks you to use it. For example, don't memorize only that one gear turned clockwise. Learn how connected gears interact so that you can determine the direction of any gear in 
a different arrangement. Similarly, learning the definition of one vocabulary word is useful, but recognizing roots, prefixes, suffixes, and context clues will help you answer unfamiliar Wor
d Knowledge questions. The exact questions on your test will be different from the ones you encounter during practice. If you can explain a skill without looking at your notes and then apply
 it correctly to a new question, that is a good indication that you have mastered it. Check Out Mometrix&#039;s ASVAB Printed Study Guide! Get practice questions, video tutorials, and detail

ol students in order to evaluate their suitability for enlistment in the US military. Q What&#039;s on the ASVAB? A The ASVAB contains 10 sections, listed here in order of administration: Ge
neral Science (GS) Arithmetic Reasoning (AR) Word Knowledge (WK) Paragraph Comprehension (PC) Math Knowledge (MK) Electronics Information (EI) Auto Information (AI) Shop Information (SI) Mec
hanical Comprehension (MC) Assembling Objects (AO) Q How many questions are on the ASVAB? A The computerized enlistment ASVAB (CAT-ASVAB) includes 135 scored questions, plus unscored tryout 
questions in selected subtests. The paper-and-pencil enlistment version contains 225 questions. Q What does ASVAB stand for? A ASVAB stands for A rmed S ervices V ocational A ptitude B atter
y. Q What is a good ASVAB score? A A good target score depends on your military branch, education level, and desired job. Your AFQT percentile helps determine enlistment eligibility, while b
ranch-specific line or composite scores help determine job qualification. Confirm the current requirements for your situation with a recruiter, and aim for the scores needed for the job you 
want. Q How long is the ASVAB? A The CAT-ASVAB has separate time limits for each subtest, with additional time provided when tryout questions are included. Most examinees finish in about two
 hours. The paper-and-pencil enlistment version provides 149 minutes of testing time; the full session, including instructions and administrative tasks, generally takes three to four hours.
