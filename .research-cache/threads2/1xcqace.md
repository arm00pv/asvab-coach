You have to know how to multiply or divide by hand to do well on the ASVAB math sections, because there is no calculator. You can find online multiplication and division tables for the numbers 1-12. Once you get those down, work on PEMDAS. Next, try evaluating algebraic expressions and combining like terms. Get familiar with The Distributive Property. Follow this with solving 1 and 2-step equations. That basically takes care of prealgebra.

Once you feel ready, try solving systems of equations. Then, work on your exponent rules and finding roots. Next, practice FOIL and factoring. You're know pretty well-versed in basic algebra 1. The next step is to work on arithmetic reasoning.

Start with fractions, then decimals, then percents. A lot of word problems may involve any one of these. You can now move on to things like mean, median, mode, range, and probability. You can follow this with an exploration of distance, rate, and time problems.

It might be a good idea to close with geometry. You want to know your formulas for perimeter, area, circumference, volume, finding midpoints, the Pythagorean Theorem formula, slope-intercept form, and properties of lines and angles. Once you master this, you should have a good foundation for ASVAB math.
