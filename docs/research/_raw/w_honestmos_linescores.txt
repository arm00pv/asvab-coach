lts — like GT = Verbal Expression + Arithmetic Reasoning — and they decide which jobs you qualify for, separate from the AFQT percentile that gets you in the door. Recruiters love to talk ab
out the AFQT — the headline percentile. That number gets you a contract. It does not get you a job. The job is decided by a quieter set of numbers most recruits never get fully explained: th
e line scores. This guide breaks down the difference, the subtests they're built from, and the Army's ten composites — so you know which number to chase. This is a guide, not a calculator. I
t explains the system and routes you to the official scoring source and to the tools that show which Army jobs your scores actually unlock. The Fact What It Means AFQT Percentile 1–99 from 2
VE + AR + MK. Gates whether you can enlist at all. Line scores Composite scores from your subtests. Gate which MOS / job you qualify for. VE = WK + PC Verbal Expression = Word Knowledge + Pa

ther branches differ Navy uses its own line scores; the Air Force uses MAGE. Army composites are below. SEC 1 Two different numbers. One gets you in; the other picks your job. AFQT vs Line S
cores The AFQT — The Door The AFQT (Armed Forces Qualification Test) is the percentile everyone fixates on. It runs 1 to 99 and it's computed from just four subtests: 2VE + AR + MK — your Ve
rbal Expression score doubled, plus Arithmetic Reasoning and Mathematics Knowledge. That number decides whether a branch will take you and which AFQT category you fall into. It is the recrui
ting gate. It says nothing, by itself, about what you'll actually do. Line Scores — The Job Line scores (a.k.a. composite scores) are a separate set of numbers. Each branch combines your ten
 subtest scores into its own formulas, and each formula targets a cluster of jobs. The Army has ten of them. Every MOS publishes the line score and minimum it requires — that's the wall betw
een you and the job you want. ★ Note A high AFQT does not guarantee a high line score for the specific MOS you want. You can clear the enlistment percentile easily and still come up short on
, say, the EL composite for an electronics job. Know which line score your target MOS uses before you test. The One-Sentence Version AFQT gets you in the door. Line scores decide which door 
you walk through. If you only learn one thing about ASVAB scoring, learn that. SEC 2 Nine subtests. Every score on your sheet is built from these. The Subtests Behind Everything The Building
 Blocks Both the AFQT and every line score are assembled from the same raw material: your individual subtest scores. Here's the standard current set. Note that VE (Verbal Expression) isn't a
 test you sit — it's a derived score, VE = WK + PC , and it shows up all over the composite formulas. Code Subtest What It Measures GS General Science Life, earth, space, and physical scienc
e. AR Arithmetic Reasoning Word problems / applied math. Feeds AFQT and GT. WK Word Knowledge Vocabulary. Half of Verbal Expression (VE). PC Paragraph Comprehension Reading comprehension. Th
e other half of VE. MK Mathematics Knowledge Algebra and geometry. Feeds the AFQT. EI Electronics Information Circuits, current, electrical systems. AS Auto & Shop Information Cars, tools, s
hop practices. MC Mechanical Comprehension Mechanical principles, physics of machines. AO Assembling Objects Spatial reasoning. Does NOT count toward the AFQT. ⚠ Watch Out Older composite fo
rmulas used two subtests that no longer exist on the computer-adaptive ASVAB — Numerical Operations (NO) and Coding Speed (CS). If you find a formula online that includes NO or CS, it's from
 a legacy version. That's exactly why we route the precise current formulas to the official source instead of printing a version that might be out of date. SEC 3 GT is the famous one. There 
are nine others, each guarding a different lane. The Army's 10 Composites — and What Each Unlocks The Composite Map The Army sorts its jobs into ten composite areas. Each MOS is gated by one
 (sometimes two) of these line scores and a minimum number. Below is what each composite is for — the job clusters it unlocks. Code Composite What It Unlocks GT General Technical Officer pro
grams, intelligence, Special Forces, civil affairs. The one most jobs check. CL Clerical Finance, administration, personnel, HR. CO Combat Infantry, armor, and other direct-combat MOS. EL El
ectronics Radar, avionics, electrical, signal intelligence. FA Field Artillery Fire support and artillery operations. GM General Maintenance Maintenance, construction, utilities. MM Mechanic
al Maintenance Vehicle repair, equipment maintenance, aviation mechanics. OF Operators & Food Transportation, equipment operators, food service. SC Surveillance & Communications Signal, surv
eillance, communications, cyber. ST Skilled Technical Medical, electronics, communications, IT. GT Is the One Worth Chasing If you only optimize one composite, make it GT. GT = VE + AR — thi
s is the one formula that's identical across every source, no legacy-subtest asterisks. A strong GT opens up the widest range of MOS, qualifies you for officer programs, Special Forces, warr
ant tracks, and a long list of schools. It's also the score most people retest or attend a prep course to raise. Many MOS set a GT floor; clearing it is often the difference between the job 
you want and the job that's left. On the Exact Formulas You'll find a dozen sites listing the precise subtest formula for each of CL, CO, EL, FA, GM, MM, OF, SC and ST — and they don't all a
gree, partly because of the retired NO/CS subtests and ASVAB-era differences. We're not going to print a formula we can't fully stand behind. For GT = VE + AR we're confident; for the rest, 
what matters to you is what the composite is for (above) and the minimum your target MOS requires. For the exact current formulas, go to the official ASVAB source, your recruiter, or your ow
n score sheet — all linked below. ⚠ Watch Out Don't make a contract decision off a composite formula you copied from a test-prep blog. Confirm the line score and minimum for your specific MO
S against official Army sources or your score sheet before you sign. SEC 4 These ten composites are Army. The other services don't use them. Other Branches Score Differently Same Subtests, D
ifferent Math Every branch starts from the same nine ASVAB subtests and the same AFQT, but each one builds its own composites. The Navy uses its own set of line scores for its ratings. The A
ir Force uses the MAGE system — four composites: Mechanical, Administrative, General, and Electronic. The Marine Corps and Coast Guard have their own combinations too. So a number that quali
fies you for an Army MOS doesn't map one-to-one to a job in another branch. This guide focuses on the Army's ten; check the relevant branch source for the others. SEC 5 Where the numbers liv

GT stands for General Technical, and it is the Army line score most jobs (and every officer and Special Forces pipeline) care about. It is the one composite formula that is rock-solid across
 every source: GT = VE + AR — your Verbal Expression score (Word Knowledge + Paragraph Comprehension) plus your Arithmetic Reasoning score. A higher GT opens up more MOS, qualifies you for o
fficer programs and many schools, and is the score you retest or reclass to raise. Many MOS set a GT floor (commonly 100–110), so it is the single number most worth improving. What's the dif
ference between AFQT and line scores? The AFQT (Armed Forces Qualification Test) is a percentile from 1 to 99 computed from four subtests — 2VE + AR + MK (Verbal Expression doubled, plus Ari
thmetic Reasoning and Mathematics Knowledge). It decides whether you can enlist at all and which AFQT category you land in. Line scores (also called composite scores) are a separate set of n
umbers built from your ten subtests in different combinations, and they decide which specific jobs or MOS you qualify for. In short: AFQT gets you in the door; line scores decide which door 
you walk through. What do ASVAB line scores qualify me for? Each Army line score maps to a cluster of job fields. GT (General Technical) covers officer programs, intelligence, Special Forces
 and civil affairs; ST (Skilled Technical) covers medical, electronics, communications and IT; EL (Electronics) covers radar, avionics and signals; MM (Mechanical Maintenance) covers vehicle
 and equipment repair; CL (Clerical) covers finance, admin and personnel; CO (Combat) covers infantry and armor; FA (Field Artillery) covers fire support; GM (General Maintenance) covers mai
ntenance, construction and utilities; OF (Operators and Food) covers transportation and food service; SC (Surveillance and Communications) covers signal, surveillance and cyber. Every MOS li
sts the line score and minimum it requires. Where do I see my line scores? Your line scores are on your ASVAB score sheet (the printout MEPS gives you) and, once you are in, on your enlistme
nt paperwork and personnel record. If you are still a recruit, ask your recruiter for your full score breakdown — not just the AFQT — because that is what determines your job options. The of
